## 3 · P3 FAILS — the call side never opened on 2026-09-04, and it is not the sizing

**Finding, and it is NOT a sizing defect.** On 2026-09-04 all seven arms opened the put
side only. The reason was read directly out of the bot Log rather than inferred.

`GF-QQQ-IC-Ride` → Log → Fri Sep 4, 13:30–14:00, `GF-ScannerB-CallSpread`, 1:59PM,
full node chain:

```
Start automation -> Loop QQQ -> QQQ
  Current market time is after 1:30pm ................. Yes
  Current market time is before 2:00pm ................ Yes
  Symbol change % is greater than -0.75 since prev close  Yes
  Symbol change % is less than 0.75 since prev close ... Yes
  Bot opened a position with call side today .......... No
  Open Position  QQQ Short Call Spread Sep 4  -719 call, +721 call
      -> Filtered: Mid price is $0.07.
  End
```

So: the call spread **was found and attempted on every scan of the window** — the gate
chain passed all four decisions, the "already has a call side today" guard correctly said
No, the order was constructed — and it was rejected by the automation's own **minimum-credit
filter**, because the call side was only paying $0.07 mid against the put side's $0.10.
Every ScannerB run in the window carries the `1 filtered position` badge; ScannerA does not.

Three things this rules out, each by the log rather than by argument:
- **not the position limits** — the limit branch was never reached, and the arms read 2/2
  (`posLimit` 2, `posLimitDay` 2, confirmed first-hand on `GF-QQQ-IC-Ride`), per
  `R-2026-09-03-POSITION-LIMITS-RESCINDED`;
- **not the 26-contract edit** — the shared scanners were edited only at
  `amount.quantity`, and the put side sized to 26 the same day from the same lever;
- **not a dead ScannerB** — it ran every minute of the window and got as far as building
  the order.

It is a thin-credit day on one side. That is the filter working. **But the consequence is
the thing the 08-31 forensics care about: a one-sided 26-contract position, $4,940 at
risk, held naked to expiry.** It expired worthless and paid, so the outcome was benign;
the exposure shape was not the intended condor. Recorded as a finding for Andy, not
actioned — OA bot behaviour is always gated.

Historical context, from the export (open-day × arm, P = put only, PC = both sides):
08-14 P · 08-17 P · 08-19 PC · 08-20 PC · 08-21 P · 08-25 PC · 08-26 PC · 08-28 PC ·
08-31 PC · **09-02 PC** · **09-04 P**. One-sided days are a recurring pattern (3 of 11
before 09-04), so this is not new behaviour introduced by the sizing change — it is the
first time it has happened **at 26 contracts**.

## 5 · Canary — P2b, what is proven and what is not

`GF-QQQ-IC-Canary` lifetime: **18 legs, every one qty 1, every one `closed`** — not a
single `expired` among them. Each closes minutes to an hour after opening, at a small
gain. On 2026-09-04: opened 0.10 at 13:31:11, closed 0.09 at 13:59:06, +$1, ROR 0.5% —
**28 minutes, actively closed, first arm of the seven to exit.**

What is proven from the Trades list: **the exit fires, on every position, and it fires
before every other arm.** That is the instrument's whole job and it is doing it.

What is NOT proven, and is deliberately not claimed: that the number configured is
literally 5%. A 0.10 credit cannot be closed at a 5% profit — the minimum tick is $0.01,
which is 10% — so a 5% target fills at the first available tick and is
indistinguishable from any target between >0% and 10% by observation alone. The
configured value lives in the Exit Options panel, which is never evidence
(`CLAUDE.md` §5). P2b is scored PASS on the behaviour it was written to detect; the
threshold itself remains unread by design.
